## Algorithms

FV1 advances cell means of depth and both components of discharge using Euler, hydrostatically cut constant face states and contact-upwind HLL. Donor-cell scaling bounds outgoing transport. Its existing rainfall, soil, drag and CFL kernels are the baseline. FV2 reconstructs free surface, bed and both velocities. Each directional bed trace stays on its side of the midpoint between the adjacent cell elevations. This avoids artificial uphill face steps. The pressure corrections at the shared cut and the within-cell bed source balance a lake at rest. SSP-RK2 reconstructs twice and accepts the arithmetic mean of its two face fluxes. Both stages must pass the face-wave CFL check. FloodWarp LI evolves staggered discharges under gravity and friction and retains continuity. Its momentum equation omits advective acceleration. The spatially uniform frictional sheet examples solve both sets of equations. The moving shear specifically requires advective transport. [Full and reduced-equation analytical definitions](../fv2_local_inertia.py)

MUSCL–Euler uses a single FV2 reconstruction and one conservative update. Hancock extrapolates the reconstructed state in both coordinate directions to half time. With η = h + z, and cell velocity (u,v), the local derivatives are

```text
h_t = -u h_x - v h_y - h (u_x + v_y)
u_t = -u u_x - v u_y - g η_x
v_t = -u v_x - v v_y - g η_y
(hu)_t = h u_t + u h_t
(hv)_t = h v_t + v h_t
```

For each cell form the midpoint conservative state by adding half a timestep times this derivative. Extrapolate the limited spatial increments to each face about that midpoint, preserving bed order. The symmetric momentum correction in each linear trace reproduces the centre momentum when the two edge momenta are averaged. Zero slopes apply at dry stencils. A predicted depth too small for both edge traces triggers a local constant reconstruction from the original state. A nonfinite candidate raises a numerical error. Donor budgets use the original available water after the first source half-step. Both cells use the same limited time-centred numerical flux. The final bed-force quadrature uses midpoint depth. The wave bounds from that HLL evaluation enter the CFL check together with the original-cell bound. Rejected trials do not advance time, maxima, infiltration or accepted transport. The face ledger receives the single completed flux exactly once. The model reuses the split FV2 source integrator. Before the hydraulic update it applies half a source exchange and half an exact frozen-depth Manning drag step. After the hydraulic update it reverses that order. Rainfall is added with zero horizontal momentum and infiltration withdraws proportional momentum. Soil storage advances through the source substeps. Rainfall quadrature samples each half-step, with forcing-knot limits applied before prediction. Hancock samples stage boundaries and manufactured forcing at half time, including the initial half forcing in the predictor. MUSCL–Euler samples the hydraulic operator at step start. The comparisons between FV1 and MUSCL–Euler on frictionless flat terrain isolate spatial reconstruction most directly. On frictional sloping terrain, FV1 and the single-flux methods also differ in bed and source integration. The uniform accelerating plane isolates temporal error when the spatial gradients are exactly represented.

FV1 low-Froude HLL retains the original mass flux, transverse contact upwinding and signal speeds. For a straddling wet fan, write the momentum jump as mean(u) Δh + mean(h) Δu. In the HLL dissipative term scale only the second term by θ = min(1,max(|uL|/cL,|uR|/cR)). This adds (1−θ)[−SL SR/(SR−SL)]mean(h)Δu to normal momentum flux. The correction vanishes for supercritical endpoints, equal normal velocities and dry interfaces. The shared mass flux retains the original donor positivity budget. This is a contact-HLL adaptation of published local velocity-jump/pressure-dissipation scaling. The proof for the published Lagrange-projection solver does not transfer wholesale to this HLL adaptation. We therefore test gravity-wave propagation, jumps, wet fronts, lake equilibrium, accepted transport and budget closure explicitly. The combination uses the same correction on Hancock's predicted face states. All code was written for FloodWarp. ## New exact solutions

**Frictional travelling wet front.** Prescribe constant velocity c, true Manning n, bed z = −Sx and tip X = X₀ + ct. Continuity holds for h = h(X−x). In the wet region momentum reduces to dh/dD = n²c² h^(−4/3) − S, where D = X−x. At the tip depth, discharge and momentum flux all tend to zero. On a flat bed h = [7n²c²D/3]^(3/7). On a positive grade let H = (n²c²/S)^(3/4) and w = (h/H)^(1/3). The physical solution is the unique increasing inverse of

```text
D = (3H/S) [(atanh(w) − atan(w))/2 − w³/3].
```

The oracle bisects on [0,1], uses the power series near the tip and integrates wet cell fractions using Gaussian quadrature after a seventh-power coordinate transformation. A second quadrature order and independently differenced momentum balance are regression tests. The time-average of section discharge is c times the same depth integral between the two tip positions. Every calibration run retains the original true-n solution, initial conditions and bed. The analytical domain is unbounded. Interior observation windows exclude a conservative wall-signal cone using the maximum upstream characteristic speed. Smaller validation windows and gauge positions are in the matrix records.

**Frictional uniform sheets.** At constant depth on a grade, velocity tends to U = h^(2/3)√S/n. With rate k = gS/U the exact velocity is (u₀+U tanh kt)/(1+(u₀/U)tanh kt). This includes rising and falling discharge. The accepted section-volume reference follows from integrating that velocity. On flat ground with uniform rainfall, h = h₀+Rt and reciprocal discharge increases by gn² times the integral of h^(−7/3). This integral is evaluated analytically on the rain and recession intervals. Pure constant infiltration removes proportional momentum, so reciprocal velocity increases by gn² times the integral of h^(−4/3). Physical rainfall and infiltration are held fixed during fitting.

**Travelling shear pulse.** Constant h and normal u transport a smooth Gaussian transverse velocity at speed u, with fixed shape and amplitude. Cell means use the Gaussian primitive. Three finite 20 m sections normal to y provide rising and receding hydrographs. Their exact discharge is the integral of hv over the section at each time. The LI counterpart leaves stationary transverse momentum in the absence of a water-surface gradient. LI requires a positive Manning coefficient, so we use 10⁻⁸ in this inviscid limit and record it explicitly.

**Damped shear pulse for roughness transfer.** A finite strip of transverse velocity +V is embedded in otherwise −V flow at the same depth and normal velocity U. Since the speed magnitude is uniform, Manning friction multiplies both velocities by 1/(1+kt), with k = gn²√(U²+V²)/h^(4/3). The shear contacts travel U log(1+kt)/k. Interval hydrographs integrate signed overlap with a 20 m section, splitting quadrature at every contact crossing. The second damped event changes depth, velocity, reach length and gauge displacement while retaining n = 0.025.

**Nonlinear gravity pulse.** The right-going Riemann invariant u−2√(gh) is constant. Prescribe c(ξ) = √(gh₀)+a exp(−(ξ−X₀)²/2σ²) and u = 2(c−√(gh₀)). Invert x = ξ + (3c−2√(gh₀))t before characteristic crossing. Cell means and interval section discharge are integrated independently. Finite-difference residual tests check both nonlinear conservation laws. This case measures low-Froude phase and amplitude errors directly.

The other tests reuse the established exact Ritter and Stoker fans, subcritical and transcritical Bernoulli flows, conjugate-depth shocks, MacDonald beds, rain-fed equilibrium, planar and radial Thacker solutions and the smooth 2D manufactured residual. The radial section hydrograph is computed independently from the exact change in wet storage beyond the section. The manufactured source explicitly supplies the time derivative, divergence of both momentum fluxes and the prescribed bed term. Its temporal self-refinement is labelled separately from error against the analytical solution. ## Measurement support

The observables are common-support interior measurements. Sensitivity to lateral and end walls in the strongest damped shear is reported separately in the calibration study. The narrow strips resolve flows uniform in the cross-channel direction, with a full 2D grid and the same continuous plane at every spacing. The Gaussian shear uses a 480 × 320 m 2D domain. The radial bowl is 480 × 480 m. The primary matrix reports time-and-space errors on the interior centreline, and whole-domain depth minima and water budgets. The radial files also report errors across the 2D wet/dry basin. True cell averages define the thresholded comparison. Extent in the matrix is equivalent wet length in metres. For a uniform strip its area is that length times width. One-sided front errors use the threshold crossing at cell resolution. Hydrographs use differences of accepted cumulative face volumes, normalised by section width. Both exact and numerical hydrographs are averaged over identical time intervals. A separate higher-cadence run interpolates the two half-height crossings and peak times. It omits widths or propagation speeds without resolved crossings.

CUDA wall times include the hydraulic loop and its normal output-time stops, with explicit device synchronisation. They exclude state extraction, plotting and input preparation. The steady timings use a warm trajectory and three repeats per matrix point, or seven repeats per profiling point. Fine-grained kernel timings use uncaptured launches and separate source, face, predictor and limiter costs. Small-grid wall time is sensitive to output cadence. The cost comparisons select among measured affordable configurations for each error metric. Interpolation between grids is unnecessary. Numerical thresholds in the tables are experimental tolerances for these small domains. They have no universal operational status.
