# FloodWarp single-flux accuracy, cost and roughness compensation

**Keep FV1 contact-HLL as the default. Advance adaptive MUSCL–Hancock as an experimental option for rapidly varying full-momentum flow.** In the 57 matched case/grid pairs, Hancock MC took a median **1.46× FV1** at common timestep caps and **1.65× FV1** under the individual timestep policies. FV2 MC took **1.96× and 2.20×**. These are measurements on small GPU domains. Absolute prediction skill on a real catchment remains outside the scope of this experiment. ## What accounts for FV1 hydrograph attenuation?

At a 5 m section through the advected Gaussian shear, the exact peak was 0.22253 m²/s. FV1 produced 0.14006, MUSCL–Euler 0.20714, Hancock MC 0.20171 and FV2 MC 0.20034 m²/s. Changing spatial reconstruction while retaining Euler recovered **81% of the original peak deficit** on this frictionless flat-bed control. At this grid spacing Euler's temporal error partly offset the spatial error. At 1 m Euler overshot the analytical peak at 0.22448 m²/s. Hancock and FV2 gave 0.22243 and 0.22242 m²/s. The 1 m section time-series RMSE fell from 0.000949 for MUSCL–Euler to 0.000347 for Hancock. There is no fixed additive or percentage contribution from time integration across resolutions.

For the low-Froude nonlinear gravity pulse at 5 m, FV1 gave 0.00615 m²/s against 0.00865 m²/s. Reducing normal momentum dissipation alone raised the peak to 0.00684, and Hancock MC raised it to 0.00779. Combining the low-Froude correction with Hancock gave 0.00802 m²/s. Across the three sections its RMSE was 0.000272 m²/s. The low-Froude correction has little effect on the shear pulse, since it changes only normal momentum viscosity. The peaks in these tests can arrive early. A universal late-peak signature for FV1 would be incorrect. See the exact and numerical widths, peak times and speeds in the [pulse table](TABLES.md) and [hydrograph plots](hydrographs.png).

## Does a single flux evaluation reproduce FV2 accuracy?

On the 5 m, 0.05% grade frictional wet front, the final boundary-independent interior window gives depth RMSE of **20.06 mm for FV1, 3.05 mm for Hancock MC and 3.12 mm for FV2 MC**. Section RMSEs are **0.02917, 0.00510 and 0.00515 m²/s**. At 5% grade the corresponding depth errors are **34.82, 3.24 and 3.25 mm**. On the truly two-dimensional circular basin at 5 m the 80 m section hydrograph errors are **39.42, 9.79 and 9.99 m³/s**. At 1 m they are **15.28, 0.83 and 0.88 m³/s**. These physically different cases support a substantial reconstruction benefit. [All matrix records](primary_matrix.csv) · [Radial solutions at 1 m](radial_1m.json)

The four-grid manufactured test activates both momentum components on a variable bed. The 64-to-128-cell orders for Hancock were **2.02, 1.98, 1.99** for h, hu, hv. At fixed grid spacing its timestep-refinement orders were **1.05, 1.05, 1.05** against the small-timestep limit of the discretised model. FV2 gave approximately 2.00 in all three. The Hancock local derivative predicts the continuous PDE inside a limited cell. It does not differentiate the entire semidiscrete interface operator. On the spatially exact inclined-plane control, the last timestep halving gave order **2.00 in both momentum and accepted section volume** for Hancock and FV2, and **1.00 in volume** for MUSCL–Euler. The evidence supports second-order coupled refinement for Hancock and a second-order time-centred source/transport update on the plane. SSP-RK2 remains the qualified choice for general fixed-grid second-order time refinement. **A stationary transcritical shock requires different limiting.** At 1.25 m the largest late-time section standard deviations were 0.08952 m²/s for MUSCL–Euler MC, 0.08378 for Hancock MC and 0.08776 for FV2 MC. Hancock minmod, Hancock adaptive and FV2 adaptive gave 0.000127, 0.000102 and 0.000102 m²/s. Their respective late section RMSEs were 0.000152, 0.000148 and 0.000147 m²/s. The MC-plus-low-Froude combination still rang at 0.08471 m²/s. Exclude unrestricted MC options from steady-jump recommendations. The adaptive limiter retains the local compressive Froude-transition test and one-cell halo qualified in the previous study. The original and corrected FV2 records are preserved. The primary matrix covered 19 domains, three spacings, 12 methods or limiter/flux variants and two timestep policies. All 1,368 runs returned finite states with nonnegative depth. The largest whole-domain volume residual was 7.31 × 10⁻⁸ m³. In a separate stepwise check across three front and gravity-wave cases, 880 of 91.46 million wet cell-steps triggered the Hancock midpoint positivity fallback. They accounted for 0.0467 of 275,954 m³ of absolute face transport. These counts exclude ordinary slope limiting and the distinct zero-slope rule at a dry neighbour. ## GPU cost and limits

The fusion experiment computes both directional slopes and the Hancock midpoint in one launch. The independent pre- and post-fusion runs returned identical field hashes at 128 accepted steps. The midpoint reuses the final-stage buffer, and its inverse depth uses the donor array before that array's next write. Both optimisations preserve the previously qualified CFL check. In the final 400 × 240-cell profile, fusion reduced Hancock wall time from 44.17 to 43.26 ms on the wet Stoker test, 28.28 to 27.11 ms on the dense dry patch and 23.69 to 22.49 ms on the sparse dry patch. FV1 took 43.40, 22.37 and 20.27 ms. Hancock owned 41.07 MiB of device arrays on the dense grid, FV1 23.84 and FV2 MC 45.11 MiB. The fused predictor used 96 registers per thread, versus 80 for separate slopes and 70 for separate prediction. The table and raw captures preserve occupancy, DRAM traffic, source, flux, limiter, capture and full hydraulic timings. [CUDA profile](profile.json) · [Fused counters](counters_fused.csv) · [Separate counters](counters_separate.csv)

Hancock MC met the 1.5× target in **38 of 57 common-cap pairs** and **17 of 57 individual-policy pairs**. On the 5 m, 10% grade front FV1 accepted 51 steps averaging 0.588 s. Hancock accepted 135 averaging 0.222 s, with six rejected trials. The corresponding runtimes were 3.61 and 12.14 ms. The per-step benchmark alone cannot predict this result. Low-Froude FV1 stayed under 1.2× across these cases. MUSCL–Euler MC had a median of 1.25× under common caps and 1.42× under individual policies. FV2's normal-policy median exceeded the 1.8× development target. [All same-grid ratios](TABLES.md)

## Regimes, resolution and equal runtime

- **Slow uniform plain.** At 5 m in the 0.001% grade, 100 mm deep rising sheet, FV1's depth error was below 0.001 mm and section RMSE was 0.0000239 m²/s. LI gave 0.00000070 m²/s. Both meet the study's 5 mm depth and 0.001 m²/s discharge tolerances on this case. FV1 also met both tolerances on the 20 mm shallow recession. Accurate water levels alone would be insufficient on the 1% routing case. FV1 had 0.013 mm depth RMSE there but 0.01243 m²/s discharge RMSE.
- **Moving fronts.** At a low terrain grade, the 5 m front with median wet Fr around 0.50 still showed substantial FV1 diffusion. At the 5 m FV1 budget, 10 m Hancock gave 7.32 mm versus 19.01 mm FV1 depth RMSE on the same mathematical surface. At the 1 m FV1 budget, 5 m Hancock gave 3.01 mm versus 6.89 mm. On the steep front, the coarser option can exceed the budget. Each affordable resolution and metric is listed separately.
- **Local inertia.** At 1 m in the weak dam break, LI depth RMSE against the full equations was 1.86 mm. In the strong Stoker case it was 201 mm, versus 32.6 mm for FV1 and 6.13 mm for Hancock. LI also incurred 159 mm error against its own inviscid Stoker reference. In the positive-friction contact at 1 m its transverse momentum RMSE was 0.308 m²/s against full SWE and 0.00000283 m²/s against its own solution. This last difference isolates a governing-equation error. LI can be cheaper for rapidly routed steep cases, but its small-domain normal-policy runtime exceeded FV1 in many slow cases. [Full and reduced-equation controls](local_inertia.json)
- **Lowest error under a budget.** At the normal-policy 5 m FV1 budget for the gravity pulse, 10 m Hancock gave section RMSE 0.000862 versus 0.001149 m²/s for FV1. The gentle front and shear pulse also admitted lower-error coarse Hancock runs. The steep-front budget did not admit any of the tested Hancock grids in the corresponding comparison. The cheapest method changes with the hydraulic quantity and the resolution options. [Measured budget and tolerance comparisons](budgets.json) · [Error versus wall time](accuracy_cost.png) · [Error versus hydraulic regime](regimes.png)

The flat functions, graded planes, analytic obstructions and basin use the same underlying geometry across spacings. These results quantify discretisation error on that geometry. They supply no evidence that a coarse raster can recover an omitted channel or barrier. Full-extent catchment prediction, coupled structures and native terrain transfer remain outside this study.

## Engineering decision

Use FV1 where its actual depth, extent and section errors meet the application's tolerance. For strong fronts, rapidly changing transverse momentum or hydraulic controls, prioritise the single-step adaptive Hancock candidate for further qualification. It frequently captured FV2-level analytical accuracy with a lower GPU cost. Retain FV2 as the independent higher-order control and for applications requiring the demonstrated SSP-RK2 temporal convergence. Low-Froude HLL is a useful narrow experiment on smooth slow gravity waves. Neither the independent correction nor its combination shows a universal advantage. Preserve LI where friction and water-surface gradient dominate and section-discharge controls support the approximation. A slope-only selection rule is unsupported. The [calibration transfer study](CALIBRATION.md) addresses whether FV1 roughness changes that decision.
