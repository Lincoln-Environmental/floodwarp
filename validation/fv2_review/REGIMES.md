# FV2 shock correction, GPU cost and terrain regimes

**Retain FV1 contact-HLL as the production default.** Use experimental adaptive FV2 for a resolved moving wave or hydraulic control when discharge is important and verification at the intended grid spacing justifies the cost. Use FloodWarp LI as a candidate for friction-dominated flow with weak advection. A low bed slope alone does not establish that discharge is accurate.

## Numerical diagnosis

The baseline is dev commit `85ffd69`. FV1 advances the conserved cell averages with one Euler update and hydrostatically reconstructed HLL face fluxes. Its production flux retains the HLL mass and normal momentum components and upwinds transverse velocity. FV2 reconstructs surface, bed and velocity in both directions, enforces ordered bed traces and positive reconstructed depths, applies the donor budget, and recomputes fluxes at both SSP-RK2 stages. Rain, depletion-limited infiltration and Manning drag use the split source update. LI advances staggered face discharges with pressure gradient, local acceleration and friction. Its reduced equations omit the advective momentum divergence. The solver architecture, source conventions, checkpoint restrictions and earlier analytical evidence are retained. [Architecture](ARCHITECTURE.md) · [Earlier analytical flow study](FLOW_TRUTH.md) · [LI verification](LOCAL_INERTIA.md)

The transcritical test holds the discharge at 1.5 m²/s on the two branches of the specific-energy curve. Conjugate depths have identical momentum flux at the jump. The exact initial cell averages integrate across the shock. Reflecting boundaries are sufficiently distant for the reported interior times. We rotated the experiment, shifted the shock, halved the timestep, removed the jump while retaining the transcritical crest, and sampled individual faces near the shock for 80 s. Eliminating the jump reduced the 2.5 m MC section RMSE from 0.04391 to 0.00186 m²/s. Halving the step did not cure the jump. At the initial discontinuity the exact cell-integrated bed source changed the MC momentum residual from 0.04537 to 0.04532 m²/s². Imposing the known constant momentum on the uncut traces lowered that residual to 0.03582 while increasing the mass residual. These initial-state substitutions isolate errors in the operator. They do not establish an exact moving-water balance. The selectable `limiter="adaptive"` requires normal compression, an increase in depth along the incoming flow of more than 4% of the shallower depth, and a broadened critical characteristic bracket. For positive flow the upstream normal Froude number exceeds 0.8 and the downstream signed Froude number falls below 1.25. The negative-flow criterion is its reflection. Mark the intervening cell and its two neighbours for directional minmod. Use MC elsewhere. The bands are numerical sensor settings tested here. For efficiency, only a cell satisfying the incoming Froude condition examines its neighbour across the potential shock. Both RK stages rebuild directional flags from their own states. The independent NumPy implementation gathers neighbouring sensor values. Checkpoints record the sensor identity. Neither the physical flux nor the ordered hydrostatic bed reconstruction changes. Moving-water equilibria retain truncation error, especially on stair-step topography. [Hydrostatic reconstruction](https://perso.math.u-pem.fr/bouchut.francois/publications/hydroproj_published.pdf)

At the finest refinement of the forced, curved-bed 2D manufactured solution, spatial orders for depth, hu and hv were 2.03, 1.97 and 1.97. Independent temporal refinement gave 2.01, 1.99 and 2.01. The 2.5 m Ritter depth RMSE fell from 21.88 mm with FV1 to 3.66 mm with adaptive FV2. Stoker fell from 30.40 to 6.85 mm and Thacker from 13.20 to 2.06 mm. The partially dry lake, including the separate 2,000,000 m datum regression, retained equilibrium. On the 5 m varying-depth Manning equilibrium from `experiment/fv2-hll-speed-reuse`, accepted discharge RMSE was 0.004979 m²/s for FV1, 0.00008175 for adaptive and 0.00003294 for minmod. The analytical momentum identity and exact cell integrals were tested separately. ## Implementation cost and numerical parity

All comparative timings use the same 16 GB RTX 4070 Ti SUPER, CUDA driver 13.2, Warp 1.17.0 and FP64. Wall-clock comparisons use synchronized, warmed CUDA graphs. Face, reconstruction, source, CFL and RK stage kernel durations were collected separately in uncaptured runs. CFL is evaluated inside the face kernel. The original `0.5 × CFL × spacing / speed` envelope, the 0.95 next-step predictor and both stage checks are retained. Trial rejection on the 12 s native 5 m replay occurred once over 125 accepted FV2 steps. Raising the timestep would address little of that cost. Full fields, maxima, soil and transported face volumes were compared against a separate checkout of the audited solver. For Orton MC the maximum absolute depth difference was 2.28 × 10⁻¹⁵ m, normal momentum difference 2.76 × 10⁻¹⁴ m²/s and face-volume difference 2.28 × 10⁻¹³ m³. [Parity results](regimes/parity.json) · [Component timings, Nsight registers, occupancy and bandwidth](regimes/TABLES.md)

The branch wave-speed reuse passed 512 seeded face cases on CPU and CUDA, with only a modest end-to-end gain. Interior hydrostatic traces can preserve velocity through the cut and construct momentum directly as cut depth times velocity. The HLL contact sign uses its numerator when the denominator is positive. We retain the dry-threshold distinction between transported momentum and velocity in the physical flux. Each stage now caches one FP64 wet-depth reciprocal per cell. Source and first Euler kernels produce the cache. An explicit manufactured forcing hook refreshes it when it changes stage depth. No hydraulic state or flux is reused between stages. Adaptive additionally uses a four-byte directional flag per cell. Sources bypass exactly zero loss or drag. Tiny exponential arguments use a fifth-degree expansion with absolute truncation below 5 × 10⁻¹⁸. The small relative-depth log-mean uses the atanh series through sixth order with relative tail below 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁷ on its selected interval. Both retain the general functions outside their near-zero intervals.

## Low-gradient hydraulics and terrain representation

The 284 uniform and source controls span 0.001–10% slopes, 5 mm to 1 m depth, Manning n of 0.02–0.15, actual Fr up to 4.56, separately applied rain/loss and three elevation datums. For uniform acceleration from rest the exact velocity is the terminal Manning velocity times tanh(gSt/u_terminal). Integrating it in time gives the exact accepted transport target at three internal sections. At steady uniform conditions this solution belongs to both governing systems. Across the offset tests, the greatest change in measured section discharge between 0 and 2,000,000 m datum was 2.35 × 10⁻¹⁰ m²/s. The imposed large-datum FP64 bed itself has quantised centre differences. The central window precedes arrival of boundary disturbances. In the 0.001% slope, 5 mm deep sheet at 5 m, FV1 section RMSE was 0.00000539 m²/s, 23.3% of a very small exact discharge. At 0.1% slope and 300 mm depth it was 0.00311 m²/s, 2.93%. The steady state can maintain a nearly constant cell depth while the hydrostatic FV1 face flux differs from the Manning discharge. Depth alone would miss that error. LI closely reproduced this shared frictional solution. [Uniform-plane results](regimes/planes.json) · [Error versus slope and Froude number](regimes/regimes.png)

Nine unsteady lowland cases supplement the uniform sheet. They use 120 s of flow, three interior gauging sections and a wet-area comparison at 1, 10 and 50 mm. Stored diagnostics include hydrographs, peak magnitude and timing, net transported volume, gradient error, connectivity, arrival and last-wet times. The downstream stage control uses identical piecewise-constant levels over each forcing interval. In the 5 m wide-channel pulse the original 0.625 m fine comparison gave 1.16 mm depth RMSE for FV1 versus 0.37 mm for adaptive FV2. Mean section RMSE fell from 0.0161 to 0.0031 m³/s. On the embankment these same solvers gave 14.01 and 12.39 mm depth RMSE against native geometry. Against subdivisions of the fixed 5 m terrain they gave 6.08 and 2.46 mm. The terrain effect cannot be inferred by subtracting two RMSEs. The additional fine-grid comparison holds hydraulic grid spacing at 0.3125 m and changes only represented topography and associated cell-averaged physical inputs. Aggregating the 0.625 m embankment terrain to 5 m lowered its crest by up to 61.7 mm and changed storage at a 0.04 m stage by 8.54 m³. The two fine hydraulic solutions then differed by 13.14 mm in depth RMSE, versus 0.73 mm for the wide wave. [Lowland metrics and reference uncertainty](regimes/TABLES.md)

On the seven existing synthetic test surfaces, the two fine formulations and the timestep and grid checks give a scale for uncertainty. The ridge and small-gap cases remain especially sensitive to the input raster. The tables include separate fixed-5 m and resampled-terrain errors. Neither a lower FV2 error than FV1 on a common raster nor agreement with LI establishes a better observed catchment prediction. [Synthetic method and refinement gaps](regimes/terrain.json)

## Equal computational cost and engineering decision

At the FV1 2.5 m runtime budget, adaptive FV2 at 5 m obtained 14.01 mm Stoker depth RMSE against 27.99 mm for FV1. For the irregular synthetic terrain, the affordable adaptive grid was 10 m, with 8.65 mm versus 4.33 mm for FV1. In the wide lowland wave the corresponding errors were 0.83 and 0.82 mm, smaller than the fine-method uncertainty of that comparison. In contrast, to reach an illustrative 1 mm wide-wave tolerance, FV2 at 10 m took 10.46 ms and FV1 at 2.5 m took 24.07 ms. LI met it at 10 m in 6.41 ms. Runtime rankings depend on the tolerance, the represented terrain and the quantity being predicted. Tables enumerate measured configurations under two fixed budgets without interpolating or scoring unrelated quantities together. [Error versus wall time](regimes/cost.png) · [Equal-budget and tolerance records](regimes/equal_budget.json)

On the complete 20 m HIRDS storm, the audited MC required 259.08 s and 321,184 accepted steps, adaptive FV2 192.56 s and 319,664 steps, and FV1 38.30 s and 263,421 steps. Adaptive saved 25.7% against the audited FV2, but cost **5.03 times FV1**. Peak discharge was 171.72, 172.00 and 139.73 m³/s respectively. The two FV2 peaks fell in the same minute. The whole-event loop totals exclude input preparation, outputs and graph capture. Earlier unpaired wall times were much longer on this machine, which is why the archived run was repeated. The native replay and full 20 m event missed the 30% development target. The full 5 m adaptive event was deferred at the catchment gate.

The native 5 m matched continuation starts from the same hour-25 depth, soil, forcing and terrain. LI takes the archived accepted staggered discharges as its initial face state. In ten minutes FV1 exported 41,707 m³, adaptive FV2 47,821 m³ and LI 49,049 m³. The final 1 cm wet areas were 1.229, 0.959 and 1.088 km² respectively. All three received 27,338 m³ of rain and closed their incremental water balances within 10⁻⁹ m³. These are inter-model differences. The canonical full 48-hour FV1 and LI records retain their original event history. The 5 m continuation cannot establish the peak of a complete adaptive storm. [Matched hydrographs](regimes/halo/wet_5m.json) · [Full-event 20 m comparison](regimes/TABLES.md) · [Historical 5 m controls](hirds48/archived_li.json)

**Decision.** FV1 remains appropriate where the required depth, extent and section-discharge tolerances are met on the represented terrain. Its computational advantage can fund better topographic resolution. Adaptive FV2 provides a substantial analytical improvement for sharp waves and resolved hydraulic controls, preserves second-order smooth-flow convergence, and suppresses the specific fine-grid MC instability. Minmod remains a robust comparison and can give lower steady discharge error. LI remains useful where independent tests support neglecting advection. None of these results establishes a universal plain/hill or Froude cutoff. FV2 remains experimental. [Reproduction and status](regimes/REPRODUCE.md)
