## GPU schedule and conservation

Select `execution_mode="tiles"` explicitly with Hancock. A full-grid device pass marks 32-column × 4-row tiles from the accepted conservative state, positive rain potential in either half-step, and all imposed-stage faces. It tests exact zero. A positive film below the momentum threshold seeds work. Rain potential includes the current PWL slope even when the rate at the start of an interval is zero. This covers rain that infiltrates completely and still changes cumulative soil history. Full-domain manufactured residuals explicitly seed all tiles.

One thread per tile reads the seed flags, adds a one-tile halo in both directions, and appends to the device work list. This covers the two-cell shock stencil, slopes and faces of wet cells, the receiving neighbour for each shared flux and the extra exterior face row and column. Cell and face launches use the same list with different bounds. The graph reads the count on device. Source integration, predictor, fluxes, donor budgets, limiting, updates and accepted accounting call the same specialised hydraulic kernels as the dense path. Each face has one writer. Limiting reads donor budgets before the cell update, and acceptance precedes the cumulative ledger.

A released tile is evaluated once more to clear stale reconstruction, face pressure, instantaneous flux and fallback state. Previous activity advances on accepted trials only. Construction, switching and restore invalidate the previous-work cache. Checkpoints store the hydraulic fields and the CFL predictor. The cache is rebuilt from the restored state. Adaptive shock flags retain atomic integer bit operations. The per-cell predictor fallback flag uses one byte. The final dry-strip schedules own 162.75 MiB for FV1 and 280.84 MiB for tiled Hancock, versus 282.6 MiB for the original dense Hancock. Dense storage, two slope vectors, split source amounts, stage states and transactional flux scratch still account for most of the difference. ## Timestep attribution

The 5 m, 10% grade travelling front reproduced the 51 FV1 steps and 135 Hancock steps with six rejected trials. Independent reconstruction of the signal bounds from the actual midpoint and slopes matched the kernel's limiting bound at each trial. An x-face limited all 141 trials. The most restrictive symmetric row was row 0, face column 41, with 0.08272 m against a dry neighbour, normal speed 3.343 m/s and HLL signal speed 5.144 m/s. The limiting face moved downstream with the front. The reconstructed dry-front rarefaction includes twice the local gravity-wave speed. Donor depletion limits outgoing flux and does not set this timestep. [Per-trial attribution](steep_cfl.json)

With directional rates `Ax` and `Ay` in inverse seconds, the historical trial bound uses `CFL / (2 max(Ax, Ay))`. The opt-in `cfl_policy="directional"` uses `CFL / (Ax + Ay)`. Each axis maximum includes every applicable source-adjusted cell and reconstructed midpoint face signal, including exterior states. The bound enforces the two-dimensional sum of Courant numbers using global upper bounds on both directional rates. The same coefficient, previous-bound safety factor, positivity checks, drainage limit and retry gate apply. The option changes the timestep trajectory and has a distinct checkpoint identity.

The alternative accepted 84 steps with ten retries at 10% grade. Depth RMSE changed from 2.880 to 2.925 mm, against 31.14 mm for FV1. At 5% grade it accepted 84 versus 126 steps and reduced depth RMSE from 3.208 to 3.160 mm. Across 57 analytical case-and-grid runs at 1, 5 and 10 m the count decreased from 10,080 to 8,773. Both policies gave the same manufactured errors at the prescribed refinement caps. The final coupled depth refinement from 64 to 128 cells per axis gave order 2.02. Fixed-grid temporal order remains approximately one. Policy choice therefore requires error as well as timestep comparisons. ## Synthetic event design

Eight analytic surfaces cover a nearly flat depression, a broad floodplain with an embankment and spill saddle, a coastal stage-controlled lowland, rolling valleys, a divergent fan, convergent hills, an incised valley with a constriction, and steep headwaters draining onto low ground. The functions use SI coordinates, a fixed seed and cell-centre samples. Positive eastward channel momentum is supplied to the initially flowing channel cases. Dry starts and initial ponding cover the other cases. The rectangular free-outlet simulations open their east edge. Coastal runs impose a constant absolute east stage. These are synthetic rating and inundation controls. Their dimensions and boundary designs matter when interpreting storage and recession. [DEM functions](landforms.py) · [Maps](landforms.png)

The primary matrix contains all 48 landform–storm combinations on identical 240 × 160 m domains, with 10 m cells. All use six hours of nonzero-duration rain, 15-minute hydrograph sampling and at least two hours of recession. The moderate plain event runs to 24 h. Each combination has FV1 sparse and Hancock dense, fixed-mask and tiled executions. Selected cases add adaptive FV2 and physical LI. Solvers receive the same roughness, hyetograph, spatial forcing and prescribed physical boundary data. LI represents momentum on faces and uses its native boundary closure and source integration. Rates in the tables are interval means obtained from differences of accepted face volumes. Peaks and wetting or recession times are resolved only to output cadence. The ledger gives storage remaining at the common end time. At each output the records include fractions above zero depth and 1 cm, active work, hydraulic-loop and wall time, capture, preload where exposed, step counts, timestep extrema, rejects, stage ledgers and the maximum sampled velocity above the momentum threshold. Fallback counts at output refer to the most recent trial. Device pool use is sampled live. ## Accuracy and choice of solver

The complete analytical uniform-sheet storm gives each formulation the same interior solution. All four reproduce integrated rain depth to floating-point precision. Hancock and FV2 resolve the frictional discharge history more accurately than FV1 in this control. The steep analytical travelling front independently shows the size of FV1 diffusion. This evidence establishes an advantage for those hydraulic quantities and conditions. General catchment storms have numerical references with measured refinement uncertainty. [Analytical solution and independent quadrature](sheet_cycle.py) · [Analytical, landform and resolution comparison tables](COMPARISONS.md)

On the floodplain, the primary matrix shows both substantially lower outlet error and lower maximum-depth error for Hancock than FV1. On incised terrain the preferred method can change with the objective. Hancock reproduces the outlet hydrograph more closely, while FV1 can have lower final-depth error. On rolling terrain several apparently favourable differences are smaller than the reference refinement differences. Coarsening can change storage and connect or disconnect flow paths. In mixed catchments, outlet and maximum-depth differences require particular caution because these steep-to-flat transitions remain sensitive to the sampled DEM.

Physical LI is the cheapest tested option on many coastal stage-controlled runs. Its median outlet RMSE over the six coastal events was about 0.0060 m³/s, comparable to Hancock's 0.0064 m³/s. At a free outlet on the low-gradient plain, LI gave markedly different drainage and storage. For similar applications the boundary law must be qualified together with the solver. FV2 provides a useful independent time integration and resolution control and can be competitive on the small hydraulic grids. The surface flux and geometry treatments it shares with Hancock limit what agreement between those two methods can establish. [Method-specific errors and measured costs](solver_tradeoffs.csv) · [Full fields and hydrograph metrics](accuracy.csv)

The fixed-geometry 1 m comparison on the 24-hour plain gives 141.3962 m³ through the outlet for Hancock and 141.4243 m³ for FV2. Resampling the continuous terrain at 1 m changes those to 166.5079 and 166.6432 m³. FV1 discharges 129.1267 m³ on fixed geometry and 183.9221 m³ after resampling. The magnitude and even the direction of a formulation difference can depend on how the topography is represented.

## Kernel costs and competing schedules

The memory audit also identified lifetimes that prevent further simple aliasing. The midpoint inverse depth already reuses the draining-factor buffer until the last face evaluation. The two slope vectors are read by both the reconstructed faces and the bed-source update. The pre-source conservative state remains live while the midpoint state is evaluated. Accepted public face fields and transaction-local face fields must remain separate across a failed trial. The face flux reuses its HLL signal estimates for CFL, and drag already skips dry, frictionless and zero-momentum states. Caching more immutable geometry would trade additional dense memory for arithmetic and would need separate measurements.

The fused reconstruction/predictor used 94 registers per thread versus 82 and 70 in the separate kernels. Face evaluation used 76, source splitting 72 and Euler update 54. All six compiled kernels reported zero per-thread local memory for both layouts. The CUDA occupancy API suggested 640 threads per block for fused prediction. These are compiler resources and occupancy-based suggestions. The controlled runtime comparison kept the production launch settings. Fusing the two passes reduced dense dry-strip time from 100.34 to 90.56 ms, tiled dry-strip time from 33.07 to 32.82 ms and tiled fully wet time from 155.41 to 153.39 ms. All matched final-state hashes. [Compiler and occupancy data](registers.json) · [Fusion controls](fusion.json)

A distinct post-source hydraulic schedule ran dense source passes and compacted from the first half-step's resulting depth. On the 250,000-cell soil, spatial and uniform storms it took 8.51, 4.50 and 15.57 s, versus 8.61, 3.50 and 15.82 s using shared pre-source tiles. All accepted checkpoint fields matched. The small gains on widespread input did not offset the larger loss on concentrated rain. These pairs used identical 15-minute output intervals. ## Engineering decision

Keep adaptive Hancock as the development default. Use explicit tiled execution on spatially restricted large-domain events, particularly when much of the valid raster stays exactly dry through rainfall and recession. Use dense Hancock where widespread rainfall or inundation occupies almost the entire domain. FV1 remains a useful operational choice on small domains and broadly wet storms when its independently measured hydraulic errors are acceptable. Applications that depend on sharp-front discharge or overbank storage merit the higher-order comparison. Keep the directional CFL option experimental.

The mostly dry 655,360-cell benchmark meets the target with a 1.02× whole-loop ratio, and all four large plain spatial storms meet the whole-event target. The fully wetted common-step benchmark also meets the target. The 48 small primary storms and large uniform storms establish important limits to the 1.5× criterion. A default change or public release premised on that criterion would overstate these results. The evidence is from one GPU and synthetic boundary/terrain designs. General terrain accuracy, acceptable numerical-reference uncertainty near controls, GPU portability, widespread-source cost and the first-order temporal treatment of fast hydraulic links remain release considerations.
