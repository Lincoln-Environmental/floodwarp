# Numerical diffusion, spatial resolution and calibration compensation in two-dimensional flood modelling

Prospective protocol v1. The starting numerical revision is `84decbb90fc4c594a19b7f4f162961a8cea140e4`. Earlier diffusion and Hancock qualification results were visible during design. Their reuse is labelled retrospective. New terrain realisations, grouped holdouts and the following evaluation criteria were specified before producing paper comparisons.

## Questions and hypotheses

| Question | Predeclared comparison and directional hypothesis |
|---|---|
| RQ1, formulation | Contact-HLL FV1, low-Froude HLL FV1, MC MUSCL–Euler, adaptive MUSCL–Hancock, adaptive SSP-RK2 FV2 and LI. Reduced numerical attenuation is expected for smooth transport with higher-order reconstruction. Shock limiting may trade local sharpness for monotonicity. LI omits advective inertia. |
| RQ2, hydraulic resolution | On the same physical area, compare 1, 5 and 10 m. Measure whether coarser Hancock attains a finer FV1 error at lower cost. Use actual tested pairs only. |
| RQ3, terrain representation | Compare subdivision of a 10 m piecewise-constant DEM with independent sampling of its continuous source. Narrow barriers, outlets and channel connections can dominate differences between methods. Report the interaction with formulation. |
| RQ4, compensation | Bounded FV1 roughness fitting can reduce some hydrograph errors on fitted events. Test deteriorations, objective conflicts and transfer unchanged to other rainfall, terrain, grids and wet states. |
| RQ5, efficiency | Sparse footprint, accepted timestep count and widespread source processing should jointly predict cost better than slope alone. Rank each observable separately. |
| RQ6, selection | A conservative nearest-neighbour estimator on independent terrain/event groups may reduce runtime subject to hydraulic tolerances. Compare against always-FV1, always-Hancock and a fitted slope-only rule. A recommendation may abstain. |

## Metrics, reference uncertainty and calibration

Depth metrics use area averages on common support, final and wet-domain RMSE, event maximum RMSE, wet areas and space–time IoU at 1, 5 and 10 cm. Report first arrival and last wet sample, connectivity, peak magnitude and interval-end time, hydrograph RMSE, mean recession error and transported volume. Sampling limits timing precision to 900 s on new storms. Report remaining storage. A numerical reference qualifies for final depth if refinement changes it by at most 1 mm RMSE or 2% relative L2, and for discharge if hydrograph difference is at most 5% of the finer peak. Apply objective-specific refinement tests, including 0.01 m maximum-depth difference, 0.05 difference in inundation overlap, one output interval in peak time and 5% relative peak difference. State any reference uncertainties exceeding these limits. Label comparison with a reference using the same formulation and grid as self-comparison. Never rank it on its own zero error.

Set physical Manning n in the exact solutions. Use the existing bounded search at ±5, ±10 and ±20%, evaluating discharge, depth/inundation and joint objectives. The joint objective is the worse of the two tolerance-scaled scores. For fresh storm fits on the development plain use a nested discrete sweep with multipliers 0.80, 0.90, 0.95, 1.00, 1.05, 1.10 and 1.20. Evaluate acquisition cost separately. Use the smallest change among tied candidates. Freeze the fitted *physical* value before evaluating the other initial state, rainfall and terrain cases; record the grid and test group. Report absolute scores and the signed held-out change. No automatic correction enters either solver.

## Timings, Pareto sets and statistical unit

Measure warmed, synchronised hydraulic wall time and CUDA graph-loop time, accepted steps, memory, preload, graph capture and output costs. Keep time-history instrumentation outside the operational timing where possible and identify its cost. Use three or more repeats for timing controls including common-cap controls and operational representatives. Report their median and range; single-event timings have one observation. Separate hardware repeat variability from reference uncertainty. Pareto dominance requires no larger metric error and no larger runtime, with one strict improvement. Analyse depth, maximum depth, inundation error, discharge, peak, peak timing and memory separately within a physically matched group. Choose the minimum *tested* cost satisfying a declared tolerance. Apply grouped bootstrap sampling of terrain realisations with all associated events, grids and methods kept together (1,000 draws, seed 24317). Report small-group limitations.

## Advisor, validation and exclusions

Use terrain, resolution, objective, storm descriptors and hardware where available. A model cannot infer actual event Froude or wet fraction directly from static slope. Train only on complete development terrain groups, select neighbourhood coverage on validation groups, and freeze before using any final group. Hold out the mixed family completely from fitting; report extrapolation risk. Never split repeated resolutions or timings of a realisation between partitions. Use a documented distance on scaled, pre-run characteristics, local observed error and runtime distributions, and a high error quantile for feasibility. Default illustrative thresholds are 0.01 m depth or maximum-depth RMSE, 0.10 inundation error, 0.01 m³/s hydrograph or peak error and 900 s peak timing error. Only recommend methods/resolutions observed in qualified groups. Report predictive coverage, false-feasible choices, missed cheaper feasible alternatives, regret, abstentions and comparisons to simple rules. Do not train on final-test outcomes.

Any non-finite state, negative depth, uncompleted recession, non-positive timestep or unexplained relative mass residual above 1e-8 stops that simulation and fails numerical verification. A below-tolerance method ranking requires independently qualified reference evidence. Missing metrics and insufficient hydraulic/reference support are explicit outcomes. Preserve failed attempts and configurations. Stop refinement at the specified study grids; unresolved cases remain in the record. Datasets, code identities and raw attempts are immutable after completion. Rendering, table generation and manuscript assembly read evidence only.
