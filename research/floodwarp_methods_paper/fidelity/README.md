## Which hydrograph is the reference?

The flat-channel pulse uses a right-running nonlinear simple wave before characteristic crossing. At the 200 m section the discharge is the channel width times analytical depth times analytical velocity, averaged over each one-second reporting interval. The `pulse-reference` stage independently integrates the transported volume using initial characteristic position as the integration variable and checks every archived section. The sharp pulse passes 80% of its volume in about five seconds. The one-second archive falls below the six-interval resolution criterion at each station. `sharp-pulses` repeats only this profile at quarter-second intervals, on the original channel and full 176-second window. It verifies the analytical transport independently and aggregates the new accepted volumes to one second to isolate output averaging. Ending a solver advance at each reporting edge can change the timestep sequence. Only aggregation of the same trajectory isolates the reporting effect. ## How the comparison works

Each value is the mean flux over the interval between consecutive accepted face-volume readings. The first time edge is zero. Within each observed interval the analysis uses constant discharge, which conserves measured transport on irregular intervals. Candidate and reference use identical physical start and end times and interval edges. A simulation timestep, a diagnostic interval and a rainfall interval serve different purposes.

- Peak and net discharge volume biases are signed percentages of their respective reference values. Negative values indicate attenuation or under-export. The dimensional RMSE, peak-normalised RMSE, variability-normalised RMSE and integrated absolute error use interval lengths as weights. The active RMSE uses the reference's 1st through 99th volume percentiles so appending zero discharge cannot dilute it.
- The flow and net volume floors are 0.00001 m³/s and 0.01 m³. Variability-normalised error requires a reference standard deviation greater than both the flow floor and 1% of peak. These controls screen ill-conditioned ratios. They have no effect on the hydraulic integrator.
- Positive time differences mean later propagation. Cumulative-volume quantiles interpolate the cumulative volume linearly within the containing interval. The central 80% duration of the reference provides a common denominator for all dimensionless timing quantities. Temporal variance includes the variance within each interval. The first crossing of 10% or 50% of reference peak uses the leading edge of its bin. Timing differences within one reporting interval require caution.
- Peak time is the centre of an interior near-maximum interval, with lower and upper delay bounds from the full 99%-of-peak interval envelope. A flat or competing peak can span many intervals. A point estimate requires a single prominent mode, no more than two adjacent near-maximum bins and at least six bins across the reference central width. FWHM uses the contiguous half-maximum region of a single mode. Rising and recession durations measure the time from t10 to the peak and from the peak to t90. Equal, separated peaks still have a well-defined volume centroid and interquantile duration. `peak_to_mean` uses the mean over the stated observation window.
- The first Wasserstein distance integrates the absolute difference of the normalised cumulative curves, including exact treatment of crossings within a bin. Its units are seconds. Total variation (TV) is half the integrated absolute difference between the two volume-normalised hydrographs, reported as a percentage of volume redistributed in time. The aligned TV shifts the model by its centroid difference and integrates over the union of both supports. It includes tails outside the original window. All rankings and advisor decisions use unaligned error. Cross-correlation uses zero-extended densities at integer-bin lags only for single-mode, uniformly sampled, fully observed events with a unique optimum.
- Signed net and reverse volumes are retained. Timing distributions require outward discharge with reverse volume below 0.1% of outward volume. Small negative contributions are removed only for the probability distribution. Raw magnitude and volume errors use signed discharge. The default background is zero. An independently known constant baseflow can be supplied explicitly. The storm comparisons use the archived boundary discharge including antecedent water. A first or last interval above 5% of peak flags active initial flow or an incomplete recession. Such records describe the observed window and cannot qualify full-event shape, timing or volume. A resolved interior peak can be reported when the falling limb is censored. No extrapolated recession or interpolated instantaneous peak is used.

## References and physical controls

The short pulse experiment solves a right-running, frictionless simple wave on a horizontal bed. The left Riemann invariant is constant. At characteristic foot ξ, celerity is c₀ plus a sum of Gaussian pulses, velocity is 2(c−c₀), and x = ξ + (3c−2c₀)t. The driver checks the characteristic-crossing bound. Gaussian quadrature gives initial cell averages and reference interval section discharges. The 40 m wide strip has impermeable walls, three downstream stations at 60, 120 and 200 m from the leading pulse centre, and an original 112 s observation window. The primary longitudinal comparisons run for 176 s in a 1,600 m strip. Every model and reference section must finish below 5% of its peak. The oblique control uses the same end time and wall separation. All gauges remain outside the end-wall domain of influence. The two Courant caps scale timestep by grid size and baseline characteristic speed. GPU timers synchronise each advance and exclude reported capture and compilation. The fine-output timing protocol differs from the normal production storm policy. In the separate two-dimensional plane-wave control the fixed 80 m aperture sees either an aligned wave or a wave at 36.87°. Integration covers both time and transverse position. Each orientation has its own analytical section hydrograph.

For the 48 historical small-domain storms, the primary screen compares 2.5 m FV2 with 5 m FV2, 2.5 m Hancock and an independently rerun 2.5 m FV2 with half the CFL. The six available 60 s small events and the million-cell mixed case also receive temporal controls at their finest archived spacing. The additional six-hour two-pulse rainfall event uses a fixed 10 m represented floodplain bed with a dry initial state and six recession hours. It compares the original 1 m FV2 against 2 m FV2, 1 m Hancock and half-CFL 1 m FV2. Domain extents, output times, input rasters for timestep checks and increased accepted step counts are checked. The regional archives also include 2.10M and 4.19M cell events. Missing references remain visible there. No half-metre grid is used.

Each objective has its own reference screen. The largest absolute change across spatial, timestep and fine-formulation comparisons must satisfy its registered limit. Peak timing includes the reporting interval as an additional uncertainty floor. The screen also requires measurable flow, an eligible event window and sufficient temporal support. Fine output aggregated to 900 s quantifies the sensitivity of actually measured peaks and widths. A coarse archive alone cannot bound a missed subinterval spike. Sensitivities are empirical; the widely used HLL-family treatments share model assumptions. The time-refinement test measures sensitivity to halving the CFL at fixed grid and reporting cadence, not formal asymptotic order.

Practical extent is reported separately at 5 cm and 10 cm. The 1 cm threshold is a numerical indicator of shallow water and front movement. An application-specific depth threshold determines the relevant extent. Historical generic inundation and calibration scores use their archived threshold and do not establish performance for deeper flooding.

The historical 15-minute spatial snapshots permit time-varying extent, first observed 1 cm arrival, time of maximum depth and missed/extra wet cells on a common 10 m support. Initially wet or first-bin crossings are censored for arrival differences. A spatial maximum needs a distinct interior peak for its timing. Gray cells in arrival plots are unassessable. The original operational archive retained internal-section discharge, whole-domain wet fractions and final and maximum depth. New reference runs and continuations additionally record depth and both conservative momentum components every 900 seconds in the mixed, rolling and double-rainfall cases. The reporting supports are 40, 80 and 10 m, respectively. Spatial momenta are block averages of hu and hv. Arrival timing uncertainty includes the output interval and spatial averaging. The transient comparisons report differences from the fine full-SWE calculation for every refinement and available numerical formulation. Fixed-bed and resampled-bed differences, infiltration and remaining storage are kept alongside the wave metrics.

## Recommendations and interpretation

Examples from the normal advisor interface:

```bash
python -m floodwarp.solver_advisor --dem terrain.tif --resolution 5 --objective hydrograph_shape --tolerance 10 --output outputs/shape
python -m floodwarp.solver_advisor --dem terrain.tif --resolution 10 --objective relative_peak_discharge --tolerance 5 --output outputs/peak
```

Relative peak, integrated runoff, median passage, outlet arrival, peak timing, width and shape each use a distinct error. Arrival uses the first rise to 10% of the reference peak at the outlet. Median passage uses t50. Propagation uses Wasserstein distance relative to reference central duration. Peak timing uses the observed peak interval. Absolute discharge RMSE remains available as `discharge`. Spatial flood arrival and transient velocity require supporting spatial validation; the advisor abstains when that evidence is absent. Integrated discharge volume measures runoff export. Numerical water-balance closure remains a separate conservation check.

The supplement uses only development terrain IDs for error neighbours. The original pre-run terrain and forcing descriptors retain their roles. The fit uses absolute error plus reference sensitivity. The runtime target is measured cost through the common recession endpoint. Neighbour selection sees only the originally specified forecast duration. The empirically determined endpoint stays out of the prediction features. Validation terrains determine the conservative extra error margin. A resolution needs assessable validation predictions on at least two independent terrain groups for that objective. The held-out assessment tests 5%, 10% and 20% tolerances. Lost channels, barriers, unsupported configurations and insufficient neighbours can produce refinement advice or abstention. The advisor does not run a pilot. Conditioned flow paths, channel-width proxies, grid orientation, storage and terrain relief are investigated separately for their association with matched solver gains. D8 path lengths and directions are DEM-derived proxies. Terrain correlations alone cannot establish an event's Froude number, wet fraction, or causation.

## Extending the recession

The original observation window and the completed recession are separate cohorts. At each end time, inspect the outlet and the same internal section over the preceding two hours. For an exporting section, every outward interval mean must be at most 5% of its observed peak, and the two-hour export can contribute at most 1% of its cumulative outward volume. Before a measurable pulse arrives, residual storage prevents a premature declaration of no export. An inward-only boundary is recorded separately. Constant-stage exchange and a wet initial condition can leave the signed hydrograph unsuitable for a positive-volume timing distribution even after outward export has ceased. A terminal tail criterion cannot rule out every possible later rewetting. The matched experiments contain no later rainfall forcing.

Every affected group receives at least four more simulated hours. All compared formulations, reference refinements and half-timestep controls advance to the latest passing end time in that group. The original section histories and accepted timestep counts are verified on replay. Checksummed local restarts avoid replaying the rising limb when peers need more recession. The original forecasts, reference identities and sampled-window metrics remain separately accessible. Reproduction can reconstruct an absent local restart from the inputs. A numerical run still exporting substantially at the end of the configured zero-rain coverage raises an error. A maximum allowed runtime is never treated as recession completion.

The rolling-soil million-cell event has independent 4 m and 3.2 m FV2, 3.2 m Hancock and half-CFL 3.2 m FV2 controls. The 5 m candidates retain their original physical extent. The archive lists all full-scale measurements, original window lengths, extended horizons, storage and actual terminal transport.

The separate `bed-recession` stage integrates fixed and resampled beds to a common horizon within each matched terrain group, including the initial-water mapping variants. The original fixed-duration contrasts are archived separately. In the operational cases both sections use accepted face transport throughout the recession. Simulations retaining no assessable outward signal can pass the terminal transport check and still be ineligible for hydrograph-shape assessment.

The `policy-recession` stage measures dense, tiled and preflight automatic Hancock at the same completed operational endpoint. It checks every interval transport and accepted step count against the explicit Hancock peer. The practical `hancock_fastest` comparison identifies the measured winning run. These retrospective costs and the frozen automatic policy have separate entries.

For cumulative passage and centroid delay, the signed difference has a second pair of bounds given by the observed bin supports. A direction is unresolved when those bounds include zero. A candidate with zero export still incurs a 100% peak and volume deficit against a measurable qualified reference. It has no volume distribution for evaluating shape.

The [application tolerances](application_settings.json) control the retrospective least-cost and equal-budget tables. Each budget is an actual timed FV1 run. Comparisons keep the physical event, station, cadence, timestep policy and reference fixed. Tied configurations retain their individual run IDs. The manual `verify` stage checks archived checksums, CSV/database consistency, eight million-cell landform groups and their common-horizon policy measurements.

The endpoint-field archive stores FP64 area-averaged final depth, accumulated maximum depth, native subcell wet fractions and the final boundary accounts for each extended integration. The retained solver hashes cover the conserved state, accepted x/y transport, cumulative rain and infiltration, and the timestep histogram where available. Dense, tiled and automatic policy comparisons inspect the same time horizon. The 48 small-event results include a paired test of how often a final-depth RMSE within 1 mm coexists with a 5%, 10% or 20% outlet error. All counts require a qualified reference for both observables. The terrain representation comparisons reuse identical measured native forecasts when their inputs and common endpoints match.
