# Introduction

A flood forecast can reproduce the peak at one outlet while misrepresenting floodplain storage or the timing of a narrow connection. Grid spacing changes both the numerical stencil and the topographic information that sets those pathways. Calibrating a friction parameter against one hydrograph can also change transport enough to obscure numerical diffusion. These mechanisms matter when selecting a method for a prescribed accuracy or GPU budget.

Local-inertia methods retain continuity, pressure acceleration and friction and reduce the cost of momentum integration. First-order finite-volume methods retain advective transport and resolve shocks, with numerical diffusion that can attenuate smooth waves. Higher-order reconstruction and time integration can reduce this error on a resolved smooth solution. Wetting fronts, discontinuities, bed steps and limiter activation change the comparison. Analytical solutions supply an independent reference across several of these regimes. [Local-inertia formulation](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2010.03.027) · [Analytical benchmarks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.0288)

The present study measures the error and cost of six formulations under common exact controls and complete synthetic storms. It isolates numerical resolution and represented terrain, tests bounded roughness compensation on separate events, and evaluates solver selection on entire unseen terrain groups. Each observable has an independent error tolerance and run identity. The study asks whether these comparisons support practical choices when terrain, forcing and the intended output are known before integration. [Prespecified research questions](../PROTOCOL.md)
